## Marginal Decision Value Ω
Celia's public architecture creates a useful falsifier for ATLAS complexity.

For every additional ATLAS decision layer or module that can affect portfolio outcome, estimate when technically possible:

`MDV(module) = Net portfolio outcome WITH module - Net portfolio outcome WITHOUT module`

The comparison must use the same information set, point-in-time timestamps, execution assumptions, costs and risk convention.

A module does not earn authority because its reasoning is sophisticated. Promotion requires persistent OOS/prospective evidence that its incremental contribution is economically positive after turnover, implementation friction, risk and opportunity cost.

Possible terminal outcomes:
- `POSITIVE_MARGINAL_DECISION_VALUE`
- `NO_DEMONSTRATED_INCREMENTAL_VALUE`
- `NEGATIVE_MARGINAL_DECISION_VALUE`
- `NOT_TESTABLE_WITH_CURRENT_DATA`

Repeated `NO_DEMONSTRATED_INCREMENTAL_VALUE` or a robust negative result is evidence for consolidation/removal rather than adding another compensating module.

## Decision rule
ATLAS is not credited for complexity merely for higher raw return. Evidence of value requires a persistent advantage that survives risk, drawdown, turnover, implementation friction and the same cash-flow schedule.

If ATLAS does not discriminate economically from passive proxies after a sufficiently long prospective sample, record `COMPLEXITY_NOT_JUSTIFIED_BY_EVIDENCE` rather than changing the benchmark after seeing results.
