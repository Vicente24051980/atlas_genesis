# PHYSICAL AI DEPLOYMENT PROOF Ω v1.0

**Status:** CANONICAL SPECIALIZED GATE  
**Date:** 2026-08-18

## Mission
Distinguish robotics demonstrations and pilots from economically scalable Physical AI deployment.

## Canonical gate
`cost/unit ↓ → battery uptime ↑ → dexterity ↑ → task success ↑ → human interventions ↓ → cost/hour ↓ → payback ↓ → deployment scale`

## Mandatory variables
- robot/system acquisition cost;
- effective cost per productive hour;
- battery runtime and charging/downtime;
- uptime and mean time between failures;
- task success rate under real-world variability;
- dexterity / manipulation envelope;
- human intervention frequency;
- safety incidents and governance requirements;
- installation/integration cost;
- maintenance burden;
- throughput vs human baseline;
- labor substitution or augmentation value;
- deployment count and repeat orders;
- fleet learning / sim-to-real improvement;
- gross margin, FCF and payback for supplier and user where observable.

## Evidence ladder
- `PD0_DEMO` — staged capability only.
- `PD1_PILOT` — live pilot, economics unproven.
- `PD2_REPEATABLE` — repeat deployments with measurable task reliability.
- `PD3_ECONOMIC` — cost/hour and payback competitive with human or legacy automation alternatives.
- `PD4_SCALE` — large commercial fleet, repeat orders, learning-loop improvement and sustainable economics.

## Humanoid adoption economics
Current high unit costs are not a thesis breaker by themselves. Large-scale adoption requires a credible path toward materially lower acquisition cost, higher uptime and lower intervention.

ATLAS Ω watches the industry path from roughly high-six-figure / low-six-figure early systems toward mass-deployment economics in the tens-of-thousands range without assuming the transition is guaranteed.

## Strong falsifier pattern
`pilots ↑ + demos ↑ + deployments flat + uptime weak + interventions high + cost/hour > human/legacy automation + payback long`

## Learning-loop moat
Physical AI gets stronger when deployment produces proprietary feedback:
`robots → operations → data → improved model/skill → better coordination/task success → more robots → more data`.

Fleet orchestration and sim-to-real evidence can therefore feed Proprietary Intelligence Moat Ω.

## Commercial transition watch
ATLAS Ω explicitly tracks transitions:
`demo → pilot → paid deployment → repeat order → fleet scale`.

Announced deployments are evidence of intent, not proof of scale until units are delivered, operated and economically validated.
