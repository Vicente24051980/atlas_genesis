Canonical date: 2026-08-12
Status: ACTIVE

## 1. DECISION DISCIPLINE / BEHAVIORAL RISK Ω

Purpose: prevent behavioral overrides from contaminating otherwise valid ATLAS engines.

### Canonical rules
1. **NO FORCED TRADE Ω** — ATLAS never needs to buy today. If only N companies pass every required gate, output N; never relax a gate to fill a quota.
2. **NO RECOVERY BEHAVIOR Ω** — prior losses, missed rallies or underperformance cannot justify higher risk, weaker evidence, looser gates, averaging without thesis support, or accelerated entry.
3. **PROCESS > OUTCOME Ω** — a correctly executed decision can lose money and remain process-valid; a profitable decision that violated the system remains process-invalid.
4. **LARGE-SAMPLE VALIDATION Ω** — engines are evaluated over sufficiently large samples and resolved cases, not rewritten because of a few recent winners or losers.
5. **NO GATE MOVING Ω** — do not round, reinterpret or override a failed gate merely to obtain a desired BUY/PASS. Examples: GREEN 4/5 is not 5/5; IR below the defined threshold is not rounded upward; NO-CHASE/WAIT is not converted into PASS because of FOMO.
6. **SETUP DISCIPLINE Ω** — execute only when the applicable engine's predefined conditions are met. No improvisational trades outside the engine.

### Source role
The Daniel Pernas / DPStrading funded-account discussion is retained as secondary behavioral-process evidence, not as canonical financial evidence or a source of security-level BUY/SELL signals. Its useful lesson is the separation of analytical edge from execution discipline. Public DPStrading material independently emphasizes auditing both technique and behavior, emotional management, patience and application discipline.

## 2. TAX FRICTION / TURNOVER COST Ω

Purpose: measure the economic drag created by realizing gains and rotating capital, especially in long-duration compounder portfolios.

### Canonical principle
A proposed replacement must be evaluated on **after-tax realizable alpha**, not headline expected return alone.

Conceptual decision equation:

`Net Rotation Advantage = Expected Return(new) - Expected Return(current) - Tax Realization Cost - Transaction/Financing Costs - Lost Tax-Deferral Optionality`

### Rules
1. Tax friction is a decision input, not a reason to hold a structurally falsified thesis.
2. A small expected-return advantage is insufficient when realization taxes and lost compounding optionality consume the advantage.
3. Portfolio turnover should therefore have an explicit hurdle rate.
4. Tax treatment must be jurisdiction-specific and verified before execution; ATLAS must not infer personalized tax liability from generic rules.
5. Leverage against securities or gold is **not** a default tax-optimization rule. Pledged-asset borrowing introduces financing, collateral and forced-liquidation risk.
6. Covered-call income is not free yield: it exchanges part of upside convexity for premium and carries execution/tax/assignment considerations.
7. Tax considerations never override confirmed fundamental falsifiers or risk controls.

### Spain evidence note
Under Spain's general IRPF framework, gains/losses from transfers are generally determined from the difference between transmission and acquisition values. STC 67/2023 upheld the constitutionality of the absence of automatic monetary-correction coefficients in the challenged regime. Jurisdictional/foral differences must be checked separately when relevant.

## 3. Integration with existing ATLAS Ω

Decision Discipline Ω is a cross-engine execution guardrail. It does not overwrite Principal Ω, GREEN CONTINUITY Ω, ENTRY TIMING Ω, Good Companies Cheap Ω, Historical Dislocation Ω, Money Rotation Ω or specialized engines.

Tax Friction Ω belongs downstream of security selection and upstream of an actual replacement/rotation decision. It must be considered when comparing HOLD vs SELL/ROTATE, particularly for high-quality compounders over the 3–6 year horizon.

### Required execution record
For material portfolio changes record:
- engine and gates used;
- whether any gate failed;
- whether the decision was process-valid independent of subsequent P/L;
- estimated turnover/tax friction when applicable;
- whether a structural falsifier exists;
- final action and evidence confidence.

## 4. Non-negotiable distinction

**Evidence > narrative. Process compliance > recent outcome. After-tax realizable return > nominal rotation story.**
