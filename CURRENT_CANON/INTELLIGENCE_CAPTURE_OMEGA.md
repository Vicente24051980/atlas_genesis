# INTELLIGENCE CAPTURE Ω v1.0

**Status:** CANONICAL TRANSVERSAL ENGINE  
**Date:** 2026-08-18

## Mission
Identify which companies convert the expansion of cognitive AI and Physical AI into durable economic capture. The engine does not reward AI adoption by itself. It measures who captures recurring value from the migration of enterprise cost structures from human labor and coordination toward compute, inference, cloud, agents, orchestration and intelligent physical systems.

## Core law
`AI adoption != AI economic capture`

Canonical chain:
`AI workload growth → capture share → incremental margin → FCF conversion → incremental capital required → incremental ROIC → durability`

Core scoring proxy:
`Intelligence Capture Ω = AI workload growth × capture % × incremental margin × FCF conversion ÷ incremental capital required`

The metric is interpreted together with moat durability, valuation and Falsifiers Ω. It is not a standalone BUY signal.

## Five capture layers
### L1 — Compute Tax
GPU, ASIC, memory, networking and other critical compute inputs.

### L2 — Infrastructure Tax
Data centers, power, cooling, electrical infrastructure and physical capacity required to run AI workloads.

### L3 — Intelligence Tax
Foundation models, inference and model-serving economics.

### L4 — Orchestration Tax
Agents, workflow execution, routing, permissions, APIs, observability, security, evaluation, memory, enterprise integration and AI economics management.

### L5 — Physical Intelligence Tax
Edge compute, sensing, actuators, simulation, world models, VLA systems, digital twins and robot/fleet orchestration.

## Mandatory variables
- AI workload growth and evidence quality;
- direct and indirect capture share;
- pricing power / price realization;
- incremental gross margin and operating margin;
- FCF conversion and FCF/share;
- incremental capital required;
- incremental ROIC and payback;
- customer concentration and switching costs;
- dependency on external model/cloud providers;
- competitive intensity and commoditization risk;
- evidence that AI economics are recurring rather than pilot-driven.

## Cognitive Tax framework
As enterprises replace part of `labor + coordination` with `compute + inference + cloud + agents + orchestration`, a portion of operating spend can become recurring revenue for intelligence-infrastructure providers.

ATLAS Ω therefore asks:
`Who collects the AI tax?`

But the engine rejects the shortcut:
`AI exposure = durable moat`.

Capture must be demonstrated through revenue, gross profit, OCF/FCF and incremental ROIC.

## State machine
- `IC0_NO_PROOF` — AI exposure/narrative without demonstrated economic capture.
- `IC1_EARLY_CAPTURE` — initial revenue or workload capture, economics not yet proven.
- `IC2_PROVEN_CAPTURE` — recurring revenue/gross-profit conversion with credible payback.
- `IC3_COMPOUNDING_CAPTURE` — recurring capture plus expanding learning/data/workflow moat and strong FCF/ROIC.
- `IC4_CONTROL_POINT` — company controls a critical interface, platform, infrastructure bottleneck or ecosystem position with durable pricing/capture power.

## Failure / downgrade patterns
Escalate negatively when several of the following align:
- workload growth rises but capture share falls;
- unit price compression is not offset by volume or lower unit cost;
- incremental margin deteriorates;
- FCF conversion weakens;
- capital intensity rises faster than economic output;
- external providers capture most of the value;
- customer switching costs decline materially;
- orchestration or model capabilities commoditize without proprietary offset;
- deployment remains pilot-heavy with limited production economics.

Strong failure pattern:
`AI activity ↑↑ + capture % ↓ + margin ↓ + FCF conversion ↓ + capital required ↑ + ROIC ↓`.
