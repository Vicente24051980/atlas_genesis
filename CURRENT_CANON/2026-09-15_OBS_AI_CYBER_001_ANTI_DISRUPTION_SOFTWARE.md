## 0. Purpose

Register and falsify the structural hypothesis that selected cybersecurity functions may become more economically necessary as AI capability, autonomous agents and machine identities proliferate.

This is not a new primary financial engine, not a valuation score, and not a portfolio instruction.

## 1. Hypothesis

Working causal chain:

`AI capability ↑`  
`→ AI adoption ↑`  
`→ agents / machine identities / autonomous code ↑`  
`→ attack surface and execution pathways ↑`  
`→ identity / runtime / data / recovery criticality ↑`  
`→ potential cyber demand ↑`

The hypothesis is deliberately narrower than `AI makes all cybersecurity companies winners`.

## 2. Key distinction

`SECTOR WINNER ≠ COMPANY WINNER ≠ STOCK WINNER`

Even if AI expands the cybersecurity TAM:
- some categories may be commoditized;
- hyperscalers may internalize security functions;
- AI may reduce SOC/tooling cost;
- vendor consolidation may transfer share between suppliers;
- valuation may already discount the economic advantage.

Therefore this observation has zero direct structural-score weight and no automatic trading authority.

## 3. AI Anti-Disruption scale

This dimension is descriptive and feeds research/discovery only. It is not a standalone valuation engine.

- `-2`: AI directly threatens the function.
- `-1`: material substitution risk.
- `0`: indeterminate.
- `+1`: AI partially expands demand.
- `+2`: AI structurally increases the necessity of the function.

## 4. Working functional exposures — 2026-09-15

These are **working hypotheses, not valuation signals and not canonical proof of company-level advantage**.

- `CRWD`: endpoint / workload / identity / detection → `+2` working band.
- `PANW`: network / cloud / SOC / platform → `+2` working band.
- `RBRK`: data resilience / recovery / cyber survivability → `+2` working band.
- `FTNT`: network / firewall / SASE / security fabric → `+1 to +2` working band.

Each company must still prove capture through company-specific fundamentals, economics and valuation.

## 5. Six active falsifier candidates

### F1 — CYBER SPEND DIVERGES NEGATIVELY FROM AI ADOPTION
Trigger candidate when AI adoption is rising while cybersecurity spend as a share of IT falls persistently for at least two quarters.

### F2 — LEADER FUNDAMENTALS DETERIORATE WITH AI ADOPTION
Trigger candidate when AI adoption is rising while leader ARR/RPO/FCF trends deteriorate persistently for at least two quarters.

### F3 — HYPERSCALER SECURITY COMMODITIZATION
Trigger candidate when hyperscalers demonstrably compress independent-vendor pricing power or displace the relevant function.

### F4 — AI AUTOMATION SAVINGS EXCEED NEW SECURITY DEMAND
Trigger candidate when measurable security-tool/SOC cost compression from AI persistently exceeds the incremental demand created by new attack surface.

### F5 — VENDOR CONSOLIDATION HARMS COMPANY
Trigger candidate when consolidation materially transfers wallet share away from the company under review.

### F6 — TAM GROWS BUT COMPANY FAILS TO CAPTURE
Trigger candidate when the structural security opportunity expands but the specific company shows persistent deterioration in capture, retention, growth or economics.

## 6. Falsifier aggregation

One falsifier candidate moves the hypothesis to `WATCH`.

Two or more independent falsifier candidates move the observation to `REVIEW_EXTRAORDINARY` and require human thesis review.

Neither state permits an automatic thesis retirement, BUY, SELL, sizing change or rebalance.

`TRIGGER ≠ CONFIRMED FALSIFIER ≠ PORTFOLIO ACTION`

## 7. Evidence gate

No observation may validate or falsify this hypothesis without traceable evidence IDs.

External articles, executive claims, market commentary and price action are evidence candidates only until verified and classified under ATLAS evidence rules.

Unknown data remains unknown. Absence of evidence is not negative evidence.

## 8. Required monitoring

Where available, future evaluations should track:
- AI/agent adoption;
- non-human identity growth;
- attack surface and incident velocity;
- cyber spend / IT spend;
- ARR / RPO / NRR / FCF of relevant leaders;
- pricing power;
- hyperscaler bundling/displacement;
- vendor consolidation;
- company-specific capture;
- recovery/resilience demand;
- valuation / forward return separately from business quality.
