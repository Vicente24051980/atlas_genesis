# CAPEX HUNTERS Ω v1.0

**Status:** ACTIVE / TRANSVERSAL DISCOVERY MODULE  
**Effective date:** 2026-08-17  
**Parent engine:** GLOBAL CAPEX CHAIN Ω v1.0  
**Authority:** compatible module under ATLAS Ω ENTERPRISE v3.1; does not overwrite Principal Ω, Good Companies Cheap Ω, Historical Dislocation Ω, Money Rotation Ω, valuation or portfolio construction.

## Mission

Find companies that **capture the CAPEX of others** before consensus fully prices the economic transmission.

The core distinction is:

**CAPEX spender ≠ CAPEX captor.**

ATLAS must search for suppliers, bottlenecks, enablers, contractors, infrastructure owners and contractual beneficiaries that convert another actor's funded investment into orders, backlog, revenue, margin and free cash flow.

## Canonical transmission chain

**CAPEX intent → funding → permits/social license → power/water/land → equipment → construction → connection → utilization → revenue → cash flow → ROIC.**

A company can fail even when end demand is real if one of the intermediate links cannot be financed, permitted, connected, delivered or monetized.

## CAPEX Hunter classes

- **H1 PICKS_AND_SHOVELS:** indispensable equipment/components.
- **H2 BOTTLENECK_OWNER:** scarce capacity, IP, qualification, grid connection or critical input.
- **H3 BUILDOUT_ENABLER:** engineering, construction, cooling, electrical, testing, commissioning.
- **H4 POWER_ENABLER:** generation, turbines, grid, transformers, LNG/power systems.
- **H5 CONTRACTUAL_CAPTOR:** long-duration orders, backlog or RPO directly tied to funded projects.
- **H6 SECOND_ORDER_CAPTOR:** benefits because first-order buildout creates a new constraint.
- **H7 SOVEREIGN_CAPEX_CAPTOR:** captures government, defense, grid, industrial-policy or public infrastructure spending.

A company may carry multiple H-classes, but each class must have separate evidence.

## Buildability & Social License Ω

A new mandatory variable for physical CAPEX. It measures whether announced spending can actually be built and operated.

Dimensions:

- regulatory/permitting friction;
- community/local opposition;
- power availability and interconnection queues;
- water availability;
- land/zoning;
- grid upgrade dependency;
- environmental constraints;
- infrastructure-cost allocation between developer/customer/community.

States:

- **B0 CLEAR:** no material buildability bottleneck visible.
- **B1 MANAGEABLE:** ordinary friction, schedule still credible.
- **B2 MATERIAL_FRICTION:** delays/cost inflation plausible and economically relevant.
- **B3 BOTTLENECK:** buildability materially constrains capacity conversion.
- **B4 THESIS_THREATENING:** permits/power/water/social license can break the CAPEX transmission chain.

Buildability is separate from Economic Proof E0–E4 and CAPEX Fragility Ω.

## CAPEX Capture Score Ω — 0 to 100

Weighted score:

- Economic necessity / causal directness: **20%**
- Backlog, orders, RPO or funded contract proof: **20%**
- Incremental margin and FCF conversion: **15%**
- ROIC / incremental ROIC quality: **15%**
- Bottleneck persistence: **10%**
- Pricing power: **10%**
- Balance-sheet / funding quality: **5%**
- Buildability exposure quality: **5%**

Interpretation:

- **90–100 ELITE_CAPTOR**
- **80–89 STRONG_CAPTOR**
- **70–79 EMERGING_CAPTOR**
- **60–69 WATCH**
- **<60 INSUFFICIENT**

Timing label is separate: `EARLY / CONFIRMED / CROWDED / NO_CHASE / DECELERATING`.

## Hard Evidence Gate

No company may receive `CONFIRMED_CAPEX_CAPTOR` from narrative alone.

At least one primary economic transmission signal is required and normal confirmation requires two independent evidence records:

- orders or book-to-bill;
- contracted capacity;
- backlog/RPO;
- project award;
- segment revenue acceleration;
- margin conversion;
- customer-funded commitment/prepayment;
- operating cash flow/free cash flow evidence.

**BACKLOG ≠ REVENUE ≠ FCF.** These states must be tracked separately.

## Capture Efficiency Ω

ATLAS must distinguish the company spending heavily to chase the cycle from the company collecting the spend with limited incremental capital.

Preferred profile:

**customer CAPEX growth >> captor own-CAPEX growth, while orders/backlog/revenue/FCF rise.**

This does not ban capital-intensive beneficiaries. It flags whether the captor is itself becoming a fragile allocator.

## Discovery protocol

For every large CAPEX announcement:

1. identify the payer/allocator;
2. map every physical and financial dependency between intent and operation;
3. identify 3–10 public/private captors at each bottleneck;
4. de-duplicate by funding pool;
5. require E2+ evidence before confirmation;
6. test revenue/margin/FCF conversion;
7. compare the captor's own CAPEX burden with captured economic value;
8. run Funding Quality, Financed Demand, Buildability and CAPEX Fragility;
9. keep valuation independent;
10. preserve MOTOR_ORIGEN and never convert this engine's winner into Principal Ω BUY automatically.

## Falsifiers

- orders/backlog decelerate before revenue conversion;
- book-to-bill falls persistently below 1;
- backlog quality deteriorates or cancellations rise;
- margin falls despite volume growth;
- FCF fails to follow reported profit;
- own CAPEX/debt/dilution rises faster than economic capture;
- customer concentration becomes structurally dangerous;
- bottleneck capacity is replicated faster than expected;
- substitution destroys pricing power;
- grid/permitting/water/social-license constraints stop project delivery;
- hyperscaler/utility/government funding pool is delayed or cancelled;
- valuation requires implausible future capture.
