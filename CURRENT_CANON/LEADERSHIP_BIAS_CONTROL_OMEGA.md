# LEADERSHIP BIAS CONTROL Ω v1.0

Status: CANONICAL HIGHER-LAYER ENGINE
Date: 2026-08-15

## Mission

Prevent ATLAS Ω from repeatedly defaulting to the same incumbent mega-cap winners while preserving legitimate exposure to proven leaders. The objective is not anti-mega-cap rotation; it is dynamic capital selection across proven leaders, consolidated challengers and bounded discovery candidates.

## Canonical principle

**Best of each house, not biggest by default.**

A mega-cap can remain the best company and deserve a large role. A smaller or newer listed company can deserve capital when its forward growth runway, incremental ROIC/FCF, earnings revisions, institutional sponsorship, valuation asymmetry and execution proof produce superior forward expected return after risk.

## Biases explicitly controlled

- Familiarity bias: repeated selection because the company is well known.
- Index/market-cap bias: treating size or index weight as quality evidence.
- Survivorship bias: assuming past winners remain future winners without re-testing forward economics.
- Recency/momentum bias: promoting a challenger only because it recently rose sharply.
- Prestige bias: preferring famous management/platforms over superior emerging economics.
- Anchoring bias: using historical portfolio membership as a reason to retain a position.

## Forward Asymmetry Score Ω — 0 to 100

- Forward Growth Runway: 20%
- Incremental ROIC + FCF conversion: 15%
- Earnings Revision Trend: 10%
- Institutional Sponsorship: 10%
- Valuation Asymmetry: 15%
- Competitive Position / Moat: 10%
- Consolidation + Execution Proof: 10%
- Balance Sheet + Risk: 10%

No market-cap variable receives a positive weight.

## Incumbent Maturity Penalty Ω

Applied only to forward saturation, not to size mechanically:

- Size / TAM saturation: 25%
- Multiple compression risk: 20%
- Growth deceleration: 25%
- CAPEX payback risk: 15%
- Crowding / consensus saturation: 15%

## Canonical roles

- PROVEN_LEADER
- PROVEN_LEADER_MATURING
- CONSOLIDATED_CHALLENGER
- EMERGING_LEADER
- DISCOVERY_OPTION
- AVOID_CHASING
- INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE

## Replacement / starter logic

A challenger can compete for an incumbent portfolio seat only when it passes Quality and Risk gates and its forward asymmetry score exceeds the incumbent's maturity-adjusted score by a material margin. Default material margin: 8 points.

When the challenger advantage is positive but not decisive, ATLAS should prefer a **smaller starter exposure** rather than either (a) replacing a proven leader at full size or (b) waiting for perfect certainty until the opportunity has repriced beyond usefulness.

## Non-negotiable safeguards

1. No anti-mega-cap quota.
2. No automatic sale because a company is large or mature.
3. No promotion from price momentum alone.
4. No replacement without Quality Ω and Risk Ω gates.
5. No use of market-cap change as capital flow evidence.
6. Institutional Rotation Ω and Money Rotation Ω may increase research priority but cannot overwrite Business Quality Ω or Decision Safety Gate Ω.
7. Portfolio diversification remains structural: best incumbents + best consolidated challengers + bounded discovery/tactical sleeve.

## Required comparison set

For every major economic function in the portfolio, ATLAS must compare at least:

- incumbent/proven leader;
- one consolidated challenger;
- one emerging/discovery candidate where evidence exists;
- adjacent beneficiary receiving capital when the value chain is shifting.

Examples of functions: AI compute, HBM/memory, cloud, networking, power/cooling, grid, industrial electrification, healthcare, financial infrastructure, insurance, defense, real assets.

## Cross-engine integration

`GLOBAL DISCOVERY Ω -> LEADERSHIP BIAS CONTROL Ω -> BUSINESS QUALITY Ω / GROWTH Ω / CAPEX PRODUCTIVITY Ω -> INSTITUTIONAL CAPITAL ROTATION Ω + MONEY ROTATION Ω -> VALUATION Ω -> RISK Ω -> ENTRY TIMING Ω -> DECISION SAFETY GATE Ω`

Leadership Bias Control Ω changes **who gets compared and how forward opportunity is normalized**. It does not issue portfolio orders by itself.

## Master rule

**Do not own yesterday's winner because it won yesterday. Do not buy tomorrow's candidate because it is exciting today. Own the best forward economics available after quality, valuation, risk and evidence are normalized.**
