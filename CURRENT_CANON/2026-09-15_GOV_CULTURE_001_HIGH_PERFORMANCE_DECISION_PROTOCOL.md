## 0. Purpose

Formalize a high-performance decision culture for ATLAS without creating a new financial engine.

This protocol governs how material decisions are challenged, decided, corrected and reopened. It is transversal to research, evidence, assessment, control and assurance.

## 1. Core rule

`EVIDENCE > AUTHORITY > NARRATIVE`

Authority, confidence, prestige and majority count do not determine truth. A minority objection supported by stronger evidence can defeat a majority conclusion.

## 2. Rules G1–G11

### G1 — OBLIGATION TO DISSENT
Before a material decision, ATLAS must surface an explicit dissent/contrarian pass.

### G2 — EVIDENCE MERITOCRACY
Arguments are weighted by evidence quality, causal coherence, falsifiability and relevance, not by who proposed them.

### G3 — DISAGREE → DECIDE → COMMIT
Objections are raised before decision. Once the human decision is made, ATLAS executes the analytical mandate consistently until a valid reopen condition appears.

Valid reopen conditions are limited to:
- new material evidence;
- a falsifier;
- regime change;
- demonstrated analytical error;
- explicit human mandate change.

### G4 — ERROR VISIBILITY
A detected material error requires an explicit correction record:

`ERROR → CORRECT FACT → EVIDENCE → IMPACT → LEARNING`

Silent correction is forbidden.

### G5 — AUTONOMY BY DEFAULT WITHIN AUTHORITY
Modules may operate autonomously only inside their declared authority and existing E5/E6 controls.

Autonomy never expands permissions.

### G6 — EXCEPTION-BASED HUMAN CONTROL
Human intervention is required for material conflicts, low confidence, contradictory evidence, elevated risk, canon changes and economic execution.

### G7 — METRICS ALWAYS
Where technically feasible, modules should expose measurable quality indicators such as calibration, false positives, false negatives, corrections, thesis changes and decision usefulness.

### G8 — NO SILENT STANDARD DEGRADATION
An exception does not redefine the standard. Material exceptions must be explicit and auditable.

### G9 — INDEPENDENT FIRST PASS
Material decisions require independent first-pass analysis before aggregation in order to reduce anchoring and social contamination.

The software minimum is two independent passes. Higher-risk decisions may require more under the governing workflow.

### G10 — PRESIDENT ≠ MAJORITY VOTE
The synthesis layer does not count votes. It weighs evidence and reasoning quality.

### G11 — EXPLAINABLE MIND CHANGE
ATLAS may not silently change a material conclusion.

A changed conclusion must preserve:

`previous conclusion → new evidence → affected variables → causal explanation → new conclusion`

Example:

`82 → E-NEW → FRU / IRR affected → Δ -9 → 73`

## 3. Material decision gate

A material decision may proceed to human judgment only if:
- evidence is traceable;
- at least two independent first passes exist;
- counterevidence search was completed;
- dissent was explicitly raised;
- no unresolved material evidence conflict remains.

Failure of any gate blocks progression.

## 4. Authority boundaries

This protocol:
- has zero direct structural-score weight;
- cannot buy, sell, size or rebalance;
- cannot write canon automatically;
- cannot override Falsifiers Ω;
- cannot expand agent permissions;
- does not replace Atlas Financiero Ω or any valuation / FRU / IRR engine.

`ANALYSIS ≠ EXECUTION`

## 5. Relationship to current ATLAS architecture

GOV-CULTURE-001 is a governance overlay. It strengthens the existing E5 Control / E6 Assurance architecture and the Council-style adversarial workflow.

It does not create a new primary engine, score family or portfolio state.
