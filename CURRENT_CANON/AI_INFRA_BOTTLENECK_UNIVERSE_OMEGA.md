## Technology–Capacity Lag Ω

Purpose: prevent ATLAS from treating announced semiconductor/memory capacity as if it solved today's physical bottleneck, while also preventing a temporary shortage from being extrapolated indefinitely.

### Core clocks
- **Demand clock:** time over which end-demand/orders can materially change.
- **Capacity clock:** time from committed CAPEX to qualified, economically usable output.
- **Technology clock:** economically relevant life/duration of the product or process generation being targeted.

### Primary diagnostic
`TCL Ω = time_to_usable_capacity / expected_technology_generation_duration`

TCL Ω is a research diagnostic, not a standalone portfolio score. Inputs must be dated and evidenced; if either denominator or numerator is not supportable, state **NO VERIFICADO** rather than manufacture precision.

### States
- **TCL-0 ALIGNED:** capacity can arrive within the relevant technology window; low lag risk.
- **TCL-1 MANAGEABLE:** lag exists but upgrade/retool path and demand visibility remain credible.
- **TCL-2 MATERIAL MISMATCH:** capacity arrives late relative to the technology cycle; obsolescence/retool economics become material.
- **TCL-3 MOVING BOTTLENECK:** the original constraint may persist near term while the future constraint migrates to another generation or chain stage before new capacity ramps.
- **TCL-4 OVERSHOOT / STRANDED-RISK:** simultaneous industry buildout plus technology migration creates material risk that capacity reaches economic production after scarcity rents normalize.

### Evidence fields
For every material new-capacity event record where available:
1. announcement / commitment date;
2. construction and tool-install schedule;
3. qualification window;
4. expected production/ramp date;
5. target product/process generation at ramp;
6. upgrade/retool flexibility and incremental CAPEX;
7. expected technology-generation duration;
8. customer commitments / take-or-pay / prepayments;
9. competing capacity expected before ramp;
10. packaging, power, networking or other adjacent bottlenecks that could migrate the constraint.

### Causal interpretation
Near-term scarcity can remain **bullish for bottleneck economics** precisely because capacity reacts slowly. The same lag can become **bearish for late-cycle capacity economics** if technology migration or synchronized buildout arrives before payback.

Therefore maintain two independent propositions:
- `SCARCITY_NOW`: is usable supply constrained today/next planning window?
- `CAPACITY_PAYBACK_LATER`: will newly built capacity remain technologically relevant and economically utilized when it ramps?

Never infer the second from the first.

### Falsifiers / transition signals
Escalate toward TCL-4 when several of these appear with evidence:
- order cancellations or customer deferrals;
- persistent HBM/DRAM or relevant product price normalization beyond expected learning curves;
- inventories rising faster than shipments;
- utilization deterioration;
- supplier CAPEX cuts after prior expansion commitments;
- qualification delays or weak yields;
- new generation displacing the target product before ramp;
- competing capacity ramps materially earlier;
- packaging/other constraint resolves and exposes excess wafer/memory capacity;
- project IRR/payback falls below hurdle after updated cost and pricing assumptions.

De-escalate lag risk when long-duration customer commitments, credible upgradeability, high utilization visibility, successful qualification and economic proof demonstrate that future capacity remains relevant.
