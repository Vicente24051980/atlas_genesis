# DINASTÍA Ω — ICAS / Adversarial Dynasty Engine v2.0

**Canonical update: 6-sep-2026**

## Status
This document supersedes any causal reading of the earlier persistence model that was based primarily on famous survivors. Earlier DRAS/Persistence scores remain hypothesis-generation artifacts until validated on ex-ante cohorts.

## 1. Methodological correction

Core rule:

**SURVIVOR SAMPLE ≠ CAUSAL EVIDENCE Ω**

A dynasty cannot be selected because it survived and then used to infer the causes of survival. The empirical design must start with a preregistered cohort at time T, encode predictors using only information available at T, and measure outcomes later.

Mandatory controls include failed, sold, fragmented and transformed family systems. Negative cases must be selected by an ex-ante rule rather than because their failure is already known.

## 2. New object of study

DINASTÍA Ω is generalized from “dynasty survival” to:

**ICAS Ω — Intergenerational Capital Allocation System**

A family is one implementation. Foundations, trusts, holdings, family offices and durable institutions can implement the same function.

Long-run success is provisionally modeled as:

`LongRunSuccess = ControlPersistence × AllocationQuality × CorrectionCapacity | LegalRegime + Sector + Shocks`

No multiplicative form is considered validated until backtested.

## 3. Control rights C1–C7

- C1 — Cash-flow rights.
- C2 — Voting rights.
- C3 — Appointment rights.
- C4 — Disposition rights.
- C5 — Constitutional/amendment rights.
- **C6 — Allocator Removal / Override Right:** ability to replace or override the principal allocator without destroying the vehicle.
- **C7 — Transformational Transaction Veto:** independent approval gate for irreversible/existential transactions.

Canonical law candidate:

**Governance friction should rise with decision irreversibility.**

Reversible ordinary decisions require agency. Existential decisions require independent challenge.

`AUTHORITY WITHOUT CORRECTION = FRAGILITY Ω`

## 4. Seagram / Bronfman falsifier

Seagram is the canonical counterexample to the proposition that concentrated authority and capital portability are intrinsically beneficial. A coherent control system can amplify a bad allocator as efficiently as a good one.

Therefore:

`DecisionUnity = amplifier, not virtue`

and:

`ControlPortability ≠ AllocationCompetence`

The system must measure whether capital was redeployed well, not merely whether it could be moved.

## 5. Redeployment engine

### RDR Ω — Redeployment Rate
Share of deployable dynastic capital moved away from the founding/legacy economic function.

### RET Ω — Redeployment Timing
Whether capital exits before, during or after structural deterioration of the legacy asset/sector.

### RDE Ω — Redeployment Efficiency
Relative performance of redeployed capital versus the appropriate counterfactual.

### RVA Ω — Relative Value Added
Wealth created by the dynastic allocation system relative to a suitable passive/diversified alternative, adjusted where feasible for risk, taxes and costs.

High RDR with poor RDE is not adaptability. Seagram is the canonical warning.

## 6. Adaptive Allocation Competence — AAC Ω

AAC replaces vague “adaptability” language with observable components:

- exit quality;
- RDR;
- RET;
- RDE;
- error-correction speed;
- capital discipline;
- preservation of optionality.

Hypothesis: AAC may explain more long-run success than legal control architecture. This is explicitly open to falsification.

## 7. Owner competence decomposition

Do not treat “family competence” as one variable.

- OGC Ω — Owner Governance Competence: ability to select, supervise and replace managers.
- OAC Ω — Owner Allocation Competence: ability to allocate capital.
- OMC Ω — Owner Management Competence: ability to operate the business.

Kinship is not a management qualification. External management is not automatically superior either. The target variable is **Management Fit**, including competence, industry fit, independence, accountability and replaceability.

## 8. Exit taxonomy

`LOSS OF CONTROL ≠ FAILURE Ω`

Outcomes must distinguish:

- forced fragmentation;
- bad redeployment;
- voluntary monetization;
- institutional survival with family-control loss;
- successful functional migration;
- conflict absorbed by governance;
- legacy lock-in.

Bancroft/Dow Jones is a canonical case for separating **ability to control** from **willingness to control**.

### CPV Ω — Control Preference Value
Subjective/private value the family assigns to control.

### CU Ω — Control Utility
Identity, influence, strategic access, information, synergies and other non-financial benefits of control.

A voluntary sale can be rational when sale premium exceeds control utility.

## 9. Outcome vector

Binary survival is prohibited as the sole dependent variable.

Track at minimum:

- FCS — Family Control Survival.
- IIS — Institutional Identity Survival.
- RWC — Relative Wealth Creation.
- SR — Strategic Relevance.
- VEQ — Voluntary Exit Quality.

A family/system can score differently on each dimension.

## 10. Legal Regime Endowment — LRE Ω

Jurisdiction is not initially a freely chosen variable.

LRE must encode:

- testamentary freedom / forced heirship;
- inheritance taxation;
- trust/foundation availability;
- multiple-vote structures;
- shareholder agreements;
- holding-company treatment;
- matrimonial property rules;
- minority protection;
- institutional stability.

Legal architecture may be a response to hostile law rather than an independent source of strength.

For ATLAS Legado, applicable civil/succession law is a hard constraint before designing complex vehicles.

## 11. Control cost

Control is not free.

`ControlCost Ω = HoldingDiscount + GovernanceCost + TaxLeakage + AgencyCost + IlliquidityCost`

Do not equate a holding-company discount mechanically with value destruction; decompose it where evidence permits.

`NetDynasticAlpha = GrossAllocationAlpha − ControlCost`

## 12. Base-rate firewall

Popular “30% / 10% / 3%” generational survival rules are not accepted as hard priors.

DINASTÍA Ω must estimate its own base rates by country, period, size, sector, listing status and survival definition.

Without a denominator, famous survivors are descriptive cases only.

## 13. Cohort architecture

### Cohort A — European listed family firms
Use a base year/window with sufficiently reliable historical ownership data. Inclusion criteria are frozen before outcomes are examined.

### Cohort B — Historical external-validity cohort
Older major family systems, used primarily for mechanism testing and external validity, not coefficient estimation when data quality is inadequate.

### Cohort C — Prospective 2026 cohort
Freeze 2026 predictors and do not rewrite scores after outcomes become visible. Candidate systems include Wallenberg, Exor/Agnelli, Porsche/Piëch, Novo, Henkel, Roche, KIRKBI and other systems selected by a preregistered rule rather than fame alone.

## 14. Minimal predictor set for prospective tests

Prefer a compact specification to avoid overfitting:

1. Control concentration.
2. C6/C7 correction rights.
3. Management fit.
4. Legacy concentration.
5. RDR (preceding window).
6. RDE (preceding window).
7. Balance-sheet flexibility.
8. LRE.
9. Owner competence proxy.
10. Sector/shock exposure.

## 15. Negative-control taxonomy

Working cases:

- Bronfman/Seagram — bad redeployment / allocator amplification.
- Gucci — fragmentation / coordination and succession failure.
- Bancroft/Dow Jones — voluntary monetization despite blocking capacity.
- Pritzker — conflict absorbed / not a clean failure.
- Bata — governance/control transition; classify only after source audit.
- Peugeot — **not a clean negative**; treat as a migration/intermediate case unless evidence establishes otherwise.

Negative controls must ultimately come from cohort rules, not this illustrative list.

## 16. Falsifiers

DINASTÍA Ω loses explanatory force if, after controlling for country, sector, size and shocks:

- architecture scores do not predict relevant outcomes;
- RDR does not discriminate;
- RDE/relative returns explain outcomes while governance adds little or nothing;
- high C6/C7 systems do not correct allocator errors more effectively;
- passive diversified alternatives persistently dominate dynastic wealth creation after risk and costs;
- apparent survivor advantages disappear after base-rate correction.

A failed model is an acceptable result.

## 17. ATLAS Legado consequence

For a small/medium family capital base, priority order is provisionally:

1. decision/allocation method;
2. correction mechanism;
3. owner education;
4. capital base;
5. simple governance;
6. complex legal architecture only above an explicit economic threshold.

### LCT Ω — Legal Complexity Threshold
Complex structure is justified only when expected preservation/governance benefit exceeds legal, tax, administrative and rigidity costs.

## 18. Canonical Legacy law

ATLAS Legado must preserve not only decisions but the mechanism for disproving them:

`Ancestor → Claim → Independent Evidence → Challenge → Correction`

The archive must never protect the ancestor from falsification. It must protect the decision process from inherited error.

## 19. Canonical questions

DINASTÍA Ω v2 reduces the core to five questions:

1. **WHO CONTROLS?** — C1–C5.
2. **WHO CAN CORRECT THE CONTROLLER?** — C6/C7.
3. **CAN CAPITAL LEAVE THE DYING LEGACY?** — RDR/RET.
4. **DID REDEPLOYMENT CREATE RELATIVE VALUE?** — RDE/RVA.
5. **WHAT DID THE LEGAL/ECONOMIC ENVIRONMENT ALLOW?** — LRE + base rates.

## 20. Epistemic status

Earlier famous-family findings are reclassified:

- descriptive/legal/taxonomic facts can remain high-confidence where documented;
- causal claims about survival, adaptability or superior governance remain **provisional** until cohort testing;
- all retrospective scores are hypotheses, not validated predictors.

**Canonical objective:** preserve the capacity to act without preserving the errors of the actor.
