# CHINA INDUSTRIAL DISPLACEMENT Ω v1.0

**Status:** CANONICAL STRUCTURAL / DISCOVERY ENGINE  
**Date:** 2026-08-16

## Mission
Measure where Chinese industrial scale, cost curves, integration and speed are gaining global market share and eroding Western pricing power, independently of China's attractiveness as a financial market.

## Core law
**CHINA FINANCIAL ATTRACTIVENESS != CHINA INDUSTRIAL COMPETITIVENESS.**

Foreign investors can reduce Chinese bond/equity exposure while Chinese manufacturers gain global share.

## Priority verticals
- EV / PHEV / range-extender architectures;
- batteries and battery materials;
- solar;
- power electronics;
- industrial automation/machinery;
- semiconductors/components where relevant;
- logistics/export infrastructure;
- strategic materials.

## Inputs
- export volume and price trends;
- regional market-share migration;
- cost curves and gross-margin durability;
- local-content/regulatory barriers;
- tariffs and trade remedies;
- overseas FDI/factory localization;
- product-cycle speed and vertical integration;
- incumbent Western pricing, margins and share;
- technology localization / IP capture.

## States
- `NO_DISPLACEMENT`
- `EARLY_SHARE_GAIN`
- `STRUCTURAL_SHARE_GAIN`
- `WESTERN_MARGIN_PRESSURE`
- `INDUSTRIAL_REGIME_SHIFT`

## Europe overlay
European auto/industrial exposure must be tested for:
- China sales dependence;
- China import competition;
- local manufacturing cost disadvantage;
- software/electronics gap;
- ability to defend margin/brand/share.

Chinese FDI in Europe can improve local investment/employment while leaving IP/value capture abroad. Assembly capacity alone is not domestic strategic control.

## Discovery output
The engine identifies:
1. Chinese leaders benefiting from structural share gains;
2. Western incumbents at risk of displacement;
3. neutral suppliers/toll-road beneficiaries that win regardless of OEM nationality;
4. local infrastructure/equipment beneficiaries from localization.

## Decision law
No BUY/SELL from share data alone. Company-level Quality, valuation, governance, capital allocation and geopolitical/regulatory risks remain mandatory.
