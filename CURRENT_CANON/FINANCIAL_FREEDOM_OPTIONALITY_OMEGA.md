# FINANCIAL FREEDOM / OPTIONALITY Ω v1.0

Status: ACTIVE CANON
Date: 2026-08-18
Role: independent portfolio objective/risk engine.

## Purpose
Translate capital into sustainable economic freedom rather than maximizing an abstract portfolio value.

Core chain:
CAPITAL → SECURITY → TIME → OPTIONALITY → SHOCK ABSORPTION → INDEPENDENCE.

This engine is separate from Return/Momentum Ω, Principal Ω, valuation engines and Falsifiers Ω. It cannot generate BUY/SELL by itself and cannot override Falsifiers Ω.

## Objective
Maximize sustainable optionality subject to avoiding unacceptable permanent-loss risk.

## Inputs
1. Liquidity runway: liquid capital relative to foreseeable cash needs.
2. Drawdown tolerance: portfolio loss that can be absorbed without forced selling or loss of essential optionality.
3. Permanent-loss risk: probability/severity of irreversible capital impairment.
4. Concentration risk: issuer, sector, factor, geography and regime concentration.
5. Liquidity/exit risk: ability to convert positions into usable capital under stress.
6. Income resilience: dependence on portfolio withdrawals and stability of external cash flows.
7. Inflation protection: preservation of real purchasing power.
8. Time autonomy: degree to which capital can replace unwanted labour/time commitments.
9. Shock absorption: capacity to fund emergencies without damaging the long-term investment thesis.
10. Return sufficiency: expected return required to advance freedom objectives; maximum return is not automatically preferred.

## Optionality Score O0–O5
- O0 FRAGILE: capital structure can force decisions under ordinary stress.
- O1 LIMITED: low buffer; portfolio outcome strongly constrains life choices.
- O2 BASIC SECURITY: near-term needs covered but material shocks can impair plan.
- O3 RESILIENT: adequate liquidity and diversified risk; most shocks do not force sales.
- O4 HIGH OPTIONALITY: strong runway, low forced-sale probability, substantial time/choice autonomy.
- O5 FINANCIAL INDEPENDENCE: portfolio/capital structure can sustainably support chosen lifestyle with robust shock margins.

## Freedom Risk Gates
A portfolio cannot score O4/O5 when any of these is present without explicit evidence that the risk is immaterial:
- foreseeable forced-sale risk;
- essential near-term capital invested in high-volatility assets;
- single-position/sector concentration capable of materially impairing life objectives;
- leverage that can trigger liquidation;
- expected real return insufficient for the stated objective;
- unacceptable permanent-loss exposure.

## Portfolio comparison rule
When comparing portfolios A and B, report both:
1. Expected Return / upside asymmetry.
2. Optionality preservation.

A higher expected-return portfolio is NOT superior if the incremental return is purchased with a disproportionate increase in permanent-loss, forced-sale or life-optionality risk.

## Interaction with ATLAS
- Return/Momentum Ω asks: where is expected return strongest now?
- Principal Ω asks: what businesses deserve long-horizon capital?
- Portfolio Risk Ω asks: what can damage the portfolio?
- Financial Freedom / Optionality Ω asks: what portfolio structure maximizes usable freedom and prevents capital from becoming a constraint?
- Falsifiers Ω retains absolute veto over investment theses.

## Evidence discipline
FACT / HYPOTHESIS / INTERPRETATION / NOISE remains mandatory. Personal objectives and cash requirements are constraints, not market evidence.

## Valid outputs
O0–O5 plus one of:
- OPTIONALITY IMPROVING
- OPTIONALITY STABLE
- OPTIONALITY DETERIORATING
- FREEDOM RISK REVIEW

NO PORTFOLIO CHANGE remains a valid output.
