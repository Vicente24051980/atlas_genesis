## Hidden common-driver envelope
ATLAS structural audit already identified a second-order common CAPEX driver:
- direct AI compute/digital infrastructure: 7 names;
- power/electrification/materials linked to the same supercycle: PWR, SU.PA, MP, VST, GEV, NXT, FTAI;
- LNG as an energy extension.

Therefore:
- direct AI/digital = 7/36 = 19.4%;
- direct AI + linked power/materials = 14/36 = 38.9%;
- adding LNG as energy-extension = 15/36 = 41.7%.

**Important:** 38.9%/41.7% are NOT "AI allocation" numbers. Power, grid, gas generation, solar tracking, aviation/power assets and LNG have demand outside AI. The numbers define a **common-shock envelope**: a severe hyperscaler / data-center CAPEX reversal could hit some portion of all those names simultaneously.

## Seat Pressure Ω
Seat pressure is not a sell signal. It means an outside company has earned the right to compete for the same economic role after the freeze.

### PRESSURE 1 — AMZN seat vs META
Why contested:
- AMZN Q2 sales $200.6B +20%; AWS $42.2B +37%; AWS operating income $16.6B; TTM FCF -$7.6B under huge AI PP&E spending.
- META Q2 revenue +28%; ad impressions +14%; price/ad +12%; quarterly FCF only $0.784B after $31.08B CAPEX/lease principal.
- Prior normalized 4-year ATLAS scenario: META base price CAGR ~18.3% vs AMZN ~15.4%.

Verdict: `OPEN_CHALLENGE`. AMZN keeps the frozen seat. Post-freeze winner must be decided by **FCF/share + incremental ROIC + current valuation**, not top-line AI narrative.

### PRESSURE 2 — CRDO seat vs APH
Why contested:
- CRDO: extraordinary growth/asymmetry but extreme customer concentration; FY26 top 10 customers ~90% of revenue, with the two largest contracting entities 49% and 32%.
- APH Q2: sales $8.8B +55% reported/+30% organic; orders $10.7B; book-to-bill 1.23x; FCF $1.2B.
- Prior normalized ATLAS base: CRDO ~25.2% vs APH ~14.0% under explicit assumptions.

Verdict: `CRDO_ASYMMETRY_DEFENDS`; `APH_QUALITY_CHALLENGER`. Do not own both at full size merely because both score well: they share a large AI-connectivity funding pool.

### PRESSURE 3 — PWR seat vs FIX
This is the cleanest like-for-like outside challenge.
- PWR: power/transmission/mission-critical construction; Q2 revenue $9.56B, FCF $0.9B, backlog $53.4B.
- FIX: mission-critical mechanical/electrical buildout; Q2 revenue $3.27B vs $2.17B YoY and net income $441.6M vs $230.8M.
- Prior normalized ATLAS base price CAGR: FIX ~13.8% vs PWR ~5.4% using late-Jul valuation anchors.

Verdict: `FIX_CHALLENGES_PWR_FIRST`, not GEV/SU.PA/VST/NXT indiscriminately. FIX overlaps most directly with PWR's construction/buildout role.

### GEV — seat under valuation scrutiny, but not displaced by FIX
GEV owns a different bottleneck: gas turbines + grid/electrification equipment. Q2 orders/backlog/FCF proof is exceptional. Its issue is expectation/execution, not an inferior economic role. Wind losses and the Q2 core-profit miss show why backlog conversion matters.

Verdict: `DEFEND_ROLE / NO_CHASE_OR_SIZE_DISCIPLINE`.

### SU.PA — differentiated equipment/system role
Schneider is not just another contractor: Energy Management, secure power, cooling, power distribution, automation and data-center systems form a different toll point. Keep separate from PWR/FIX when deduplicating.

Verdict: `DEFEND_ROLE / VALUATION_CHECK`.

### VST — generator/contractual-power role
Vistra is an owner/operator of generation and retail power, not an equipment supplier. Its economics depend on power prices, capacity markets, contracts, acquisitions and leverage in addition to data-center load growth.

Verdict: `SEPARATE_POWER_POOL`; do not automatically deduplicate it against PWR/FIX.

### NXT — clean-power platform
Nextpower's record Q1 FY27 revenue/backlog and improving margin support a separate clean-generation construction/technology role.

Verdict: `DEFEND_ROLE / POLICY_AND_PROJECT_GATE`.
