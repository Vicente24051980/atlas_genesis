## Falsifiable question
Can conversational AI convert high-intent user interactions into repeatable advertising economics with positive contribution margin while preserving trust, retention and answer independence?

## Primary evidence packet
1. OpenAI Help Center — Ads in ChatGPT: ads may appear for Free and Go; Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu remain ad-free. Ads do not influence answers, are separated and clearly labeled, and advertisers do not receive chats/history/memories/personal data.
   - https://help.openai.com/es-es/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt
2. OpenAI — New ways to buy ChatGPT ads (2026-05-05): CPM + CPC buying, beta self-serve Ads Manager, pixel measurement and Conversions API; aggregated performance measurement without giving advertisers individual conversations.
   - https://openai.com/index/new-ways-to-buy-chatgpt-ads/
3. OpenAI — Testing ads in ChatGPT (updated 2026-08-11): ads launched in the United States and subsequently in the United Kingdom, Mexico, Brazil, Japan and South Korea; expansion is phased.
   - https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/

## FACTS
- Advertising inventory is operational in multiple ChatGPT markets.
- Eligible inventory is associated with Free/Go access; higher paid tiers listed above remain ad-free.
- OpenAI publicly commits to answer independence and separation between ads and model responses.
- Advertiser buying infrastructure exists through CPM/CPC and Ads Manager.
- Conversion measurement exists through pixel and Conversions API.
- Advertisers receive aggregated performance information, not users' individual conversations.

## HYPOTHESES
- Decision-oriented conversational context may generate higher-quality commercial intent than generic display inventory.
- Better conversion measurement may increase performance-advertiser budgets if ROAS is competitive.
- Advertising could offset a portion of inference/service cost for Free/Go users.
- A durable conversational-intent control point could become an AI Tollbooth if monetization scales without materially degrading retention/trust.

## NOT PROVEN
- Material company-specific ad revenue.
- Revenue per eligible user.
- Fill rate or ad load economics.
- Incremental contribution margin after inference/service cost.
- Repeat advertiser ROAS and retention.
- User-retention impact from ad exposure.
- Durable share of commercial intent versus search, marketplaces, social platforms or competing assistants.

It remains below `C3_REVENUE_LINKAGE` because public evidence does not yet establish sufficient company-specific advertising revenue linkage.

## Next evidence required
- disclosed advertising revenue or traceable revenue bridge;
- eligible-user base and ad impressions/fill rate;
- revenue per eligible user;
- inference/service cost per eligible user;
- incremental contribution margin;
- advertiser repeat spend and ROAS;
- retention/engagement/trust impact for ad-exposed cohorts;
- market-by-market privacy/regulatory economics;
- evidence of durable intent capture versus search/social/commerce competitors.

## Red Team
A positive advertising rollout is not automatically good economics. The thesis weakens if ad load reduces retention, privacy/regulatory constraints materially limit monetizable inventory, conversion quality is weak, advertiser budgets fail to repeat, or serving/inference costs absorb the incremental revenue.
