## Key hard evidence

### AI buildout magnitude
Morgan Stanley estimates roughly $800B of AI infrastructure spending in 2026 and $1.1T in 2027; it also estimates ~$2.9T of global data-center construction cost through 2028. Demand has continued despite sharp rises in memory, turbine and financing costs.
Sources:
- https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/podcasts/thoughts-on-the-market/ai-spending-inelastic-andrew-sheets
- https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/ai-market-trends-institute-2026

### Memory scarcity
Morgan Stanley estimates servers could rise from 37% of DRAM demand in 2023 to 59% by 2028 and enterprise SSD from 18% to 65% of NAND demand. This supports a structural mix shift, but does not remove the industry's supply-cycle risk.
Source: https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/podcasts/thoughts-on-the-market/high-cost-of-AI-memory-shawn-kim

## The main conclusion
**The money is not going to one sector.**

The strongest *economic* destination cluster is currently:
`GRID → POWER → COOLING → DATA-CENTER CONSTRUCTION → MEMORY/NETWORKING → SEMI EQUIPMENT`.

The strongest *verified asset-class flows* are currently:
`MONEY MARKETS + BONDS + EUROPE EQUITIES + GLOBAL EQUITIES + GOLD`, with smaller verified flows into energy and staples.

Software is currently a **capital-migration early signal**, not a confirmed public-flow winner. Building materials and transport remain second/third-order hypotheses until order/revenue/FCF evidence proves transmission.

## Decision law
A destination can be economically strong and still be a bad stock trade because of valuation/crowding. A destination can receive private capital before public flows arrive. A price rally can confirm timing but cannot create a flow claim. Portfolio Construction Ω must deduplicate the common hyperscaler funding pool before assigning weights.
