## Executive finding

The strongest current CAPEX capture is not confined to compute. The hard economic evidence is broadening into **power generation, grid, electrical equipment, thermal management, mechanical/electrical construction, interconnect and energy technology**.

The preferred profile is:

**externally funded CAPEX river → orders/book-to-bill → backlog/RPO → revenue → margin → OCF/FCF, while the supplier's own CAPEX burden remains controlled relative to captured demand.**

This audit deliberately separates structural capture from price/valuation.

## What is actually scarce

The research suggests five constraints where the next dollar of AI/industrial CAPEX is increasingly forced to pass:

1. **Generation capacity:** gas turbines and dispatchable power — GEV, BKR.
2. **Grid/transmission/electrical distribution:** PWR, ETN, SU.PA, HUBB, POWL, NVT.
3. **Thermal management:** VRT, TT, JCI, NVT, BKR/Chart.
4. **Skilled physical deployment:** PWR, FIX, EME.
5. **High-speed connectivity/manufacturing chokepoints:** APH, ANET, CRDO, GLW/LITE, ASML/KLAC.

These constraints are economically different. ATLAS must not count multiple products financed by the same hyperscaler pool as independent CAPEX convergence.

## Buildability & Social License Ω

Buildability is now a first-class gate because announced AI/data-center capacity can fail to become operating capacity through:

- interconnection queues;
- insufficient generation/transmission;
- transformer/switchgear lead times;
- local permitting and zoning;
- water constraints;
- community opposition;
- cost allocation for grid upgrades.

The key second-order inference is that **a bottleneck for the spender can be a revenue pool for the solver**. Therefore Buildability risk is not only a negative filter: it is also a discovery source for H6 SECOND_ORDER_CAPTOR candidates.

## Contract-quality warning

Quanta's disclosed backlog includes estimated orders under MSAs and certain non-fixed-price contracts. Therefore ATLAS records PWR backlog and RPO separately. Similar rules apply everywhere:

**contracted RPO > estimated backlog > management pipeline > TAM narrative** in evidentiary strength, all else equal.

## Immediate next gate: valuation and expectations

No Tier A company is an automatic BUY. The next pipeline is:

**CAPEX Capture confirmed → Capital Funding Quality → incremental ROIC / Capital Efficiency → valuation / implied return → Entry Timing → Portfolio correlation.**

A company can be an ELITE_CAPTOR and still be a poor investment at a price that discounts perfect execution.
