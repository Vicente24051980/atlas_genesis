# ATLAS Ω — Sector-Agnostic Money Map

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Mode:** evidence-first, sector-agnostic

## Current verified money rivers

### 1. AI / data-center physical buildout — VERY STRONG
Capital is still accelerating into hyperscaler compute and the physical stack around it. Power demand, grid interconnection, cooling, electrical distribution and construction remain hard bottlenecks.

Priority captors for deeper valuation work: GEV, ETN, PWR, TT, VRT, APH, BKR, FIX, POWL, SU.PA.

### 2. Power / grid / generation — VERY STRONG
US electricity consumption is expected to set records in 2026 and 2027. The investment pool is broader than AI alone: utilities, industrial loads, electrification, energy security and data centers converge here.

Priority captors: GEV, ETN, PWR, SU.PA, POWL, HUBB, CEG/VST where generation economics pass their independent gates.

### 3. Aerospace + defense — VERY STRONG
RTX reported Q2 2026 backlog of $289B, split between $170B commercial aerospace and $119B defense. General Dynamics reported $136.5B backlog and $20B of Q2 orders. GE Aerospace reported Q2 orders of $16.5B, revenue +21% and FCF +43%.

Priority captors: RTX, GE, HWM, GD. Do not equate budget growth with buy signal; execution and valuation remain separate.

### 4. Healthcare manufacturing / GLP-1 capacity — STRONG
Lilly continues a multiyear manufacturing buildout, including an additional $4.5B in Indiana and a $3.5B Pennsylvania injectable/device site. Novo Nordisk guides to roughly DKK55B of 2026 capex and continues global API, aseptic, finished-dose and packaging expansion.

Priority economic question: which suppliers of sterile fill-finish, automation, process equipment, packaging and construction capture this capex with better capital intensity than the drug owners themselves.

Potential supplier layer for research: TMO, DHR, WST, ROP and selected engineering/automation names; evidence must be project-specific before confirmation.

### 5. Consumer staples — REAL MONEY, BUT NOT CURRENTLY THE STRONGEST CAPEX RIVER
Coca-Cola expects roughly $2.2B of 2026 capex. PepsiCo expects capital spending below 5% of net revenue and is restructuring its North American supply chain. This is economically real, but current evidence does not show a capex acceleration comparable with data centers, grid, aerospace/defense or GLP-1 manufacturing.

KO/PEP therefore remain eligible under the sector-agnostic law, but they do not receive priority merely because they are defensive or high-quality. They can still win through valuation, operating cash generation, margin expansion or a turnaround engine rather than CAPEX Hunters Ω.

### 6. Infrastructure finance — ACCELERATING FUNDING SOURCE
Bank of America has announced a $250B U.S. critical-infrastructure financing initiative across digital and physical infrastructure. This is funding availability, not end-demand proof, but it strengthens the transmission capacity of data-center, energy, transport and natural-gas projects.

## Cross-river shortlist for final economic filter

Tier A — strongest verified money capture today: GEV, ETN, PWR, TT, VRT, RTX, GE, HWM.

Tier B — strong/interesting but needs one more valuation or proof layer: APH, BKR, FIX, POWL, SU.PA, GD, HUBB.

Tier C — sector-agnostic comparison names: KO, PEP, LLY, NVO. These are not rejected; they compete on their own economics, valuation and implied return rather than thematic preference.

## Decision rule

A company can displace a current Tier A name regardless of sector if it demonstrates superior:

Economic proof + growth durability + FCF/share + incremental ROIC + funding robustness + valuation/implied return.

No sector has a reserved seat.
