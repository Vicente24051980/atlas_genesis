## Canada gate — materially de-risked, but not revenue yet
Primary TKMS evidence dated 6-Jul-2026 confirms Team 212CD was selected as Preferred Supplier for the Canadian Patrol Submarine Project, potentially covering 12 submarines. TKMS states that successful conversion would increase the current order backlog by more than 50%, with first boat delivery targeted by 2033.

Interpretation:
- The strategic win is now VERIFIED FACT, not merely tender speculation.
- Contract negotiations, industrial planning and implementation remain ahead; therefore ATLAS does not yet book Canada as signed backlog/revenue.
- Canada meaningfully raises the probability of the bull case and supports capacity expansion, but economics, payment milestones, contract margin and final order recognition remain Pending Primary Validation.

## Margin gate — direction validated, >7% not yet achieved
Primary company guidance repeatedly targets adjusted EBIT margin >7% in the medium term, supported by:
- legacy-project roll-off in Submarines;
- higher share of newer, better-margin programs;
- process optimization;
- capacity utilization and scale;
- increasing contribution from electronics/sensors/technology solutions.

H1 FY2025/26 margin was 5.1%; FY2024/25 was 6.0%. The secondary Q3 data indicate 6.8% in Q3 and FY guidance up to 6.5%, which is encouraging but insufficient to declare the >7% gate passed. ATLAS requires either a reported full-year/TTM trajectory above ~7% or company guidance/contract mix that makes sustained >7% demonstrably probable.

## FCF / cash-conversion gate — still open
Reported FY2024/25 FCF of €784m is unsuitable as a steady-state valuation anchor because large customer prepayments drive working-capital timing. H1 FY2025/26 FCF of -€72m demonstrates the reverse effect during execution.

ATLAS treatment:
- Do not value TKMS on one-year FCF yield.
- Use multi-year cash conversion across project cycles.
- Separate customer advances from economic owner earnings.
- Track capex funded by customers versus TKMS-funded capacity expansion.
- The company planned ~€200m strategic investment in FY2025/26, with a substantial part expected to be customer-funded; medium-term investment intensity is targeted at ~4% of sales.

Cash-conversion gate remains NOT PASSED until a multi-year post-spin dataset exists or project-level cash economics provide a robust normalized proxy.

## Valuation snapshot and sanity check
Reference price: €94.20.
Shares outstanding: 63.523647m.
Implied equity value: ~€5.98bn.

At FY2024/25 net profit €108m, TKMS trades at ~55x trailing earnings.
At current company/IR consensus FY26 adjusted EBIT of ~€146m, equity value / adjusted EBIT is ~41x before any enterprise-value adjustment.

This matters: TKMS is not cheap on current earnings. The investment case depends on substantial EBIT growth from backlog conversion, margin expansion and major contract wins.

### Medium-term company-guidance sanity check
Starting from ~€2.2bn FY2024/25 sales and using the company's ~10% medium-term annual growth ambition, a simple four-to-five-year path reaches roughly €3.2–3.5bn sales before any transformative Canada uplift. At 7–8% EBIT margin, that implies roughly €225–280m adjusted EBIT.

Against ~€5.98bn current equity value, that still represents a demanding multiple. Therefore a €105–125 base valuation cannot be justified solely by the company's generic 10% growth / >7% margin targets. It requires stronger assumptions: faster growth, materially higher margins, substantial Canada economics, higher-value electronics mix, or a premium defense-sector multiple.

This is an important ATLAS correction: the operating thesis strengthened, but the valuation case is less forgiving than a simple backlog narrative suggests.

## Bull/Bear Adversarial Review Ω
### Bull case
- Canada converts into the largest order in TKMS history and raises backlog >50%.
- Legacy submarine contracts roll off and new 212CD economics lift margins.
- Electronics mix rises and group margin moves sustainably above 8%.
- Maritime-defense spending remains structurally elevated.
- TKMS compounds EBIT materially faster than revenue through operating leverage.

### Bear case
- Canada is delayed, downsized or carries weaker-than-expected economics.
- Revenue grows but long-duration project costs prevent meaningful margin expansion.
- FCF remains highly volatile and capital intensity rises with capacity expansion.
- Current valuation multiple compresses before EBIT catches up.
- Atlas Electronics margin pressure persists.

### Decisive evidence
The decisive variable is no longer demand. Demand/backlog/moat are strong. The decisive variable is whether TKMS can convert the backlog into sustained high-single-digit margins and normalized cash earnings quickly enough to justify the current valuation.
