# ATLAS Ω — Leadership Bias Control Ω implementation

Date: 2026-08-15

## Decision

ATLAS must not systematically collapse discovery and portfolio review into the same mega-cap incumbents. Size, index weight, fame and historical performance are not independent evidence of future expected return.

At the same time, ATLAS must not adopt an anti-mega-cap bias. Proven leaders remain eligible whenever their forward economics continue to dominate alternatives.

## New canonical behavior

Every material portfolio function must compare:

1. Proven incumbent leader.
2. Consolidated challenger.
3. Emerging leader / discovery option where evidence permits.
4. Adjacent beneficiary when capital is migrating along the value chain.

The comparison is normalized using Forward Asymmetry Score Ω and Incumbent Maturity Penalty Ω, then passed through Quality, Valuation, Risk, Institutional Rotation, Entry Timing and Decision Safety gates.

## Desired portfolio philosophy

**Best of each house.**

The portfolio may simultaneously hold:

- elite mega-cap compounders;
- mid-cap or large-cap challengers already proving execution;
- smaller high-growth names at smaller weights when uncertainty is materially higher.

The engine explicitly supports STARTER_CHALLENGER as a middle state so ATLAS does not wait for complete certainty until a re-rating has already removed the asymmetry.

## Anti-bias objective

The engine addresses familiarity, market-cap/index, survivorship, prestige, anchoring and recency/momentum biases without deleting legitimate structural leadership.

## Safety

No automatic portfolio order. A challenger can compete for replacement only after Quality/Risk gates and a material forward-asymmetry advantage. Momentum alone cannot promote a security.
