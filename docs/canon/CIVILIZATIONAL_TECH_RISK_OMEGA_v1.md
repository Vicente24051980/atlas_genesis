Civilizational Tech Risk Ω tracks how technology amplifies human incentives: surveillance, attention capture, information abundance, payment rails, AI opacity, synthetic reality and social control.

Canonical sentence:

> Science fiction did not only predict technologies; it predicted human incentives amplified by technology.

## 4. Core analytical axes

Civilizational Tech Risk Ω evaluates:

1. Who controls the infrastructure?
2. Who controls identity and payments?
3. Who controls attention and recommendation?
4. Who controls historical memory and language?
5. Can users verify reality, source and provenance?
6. Are incentives aligned with human agency or dependency?
7. Does the system decentralize power or concentrate it?
8. Is the technology a tool, a gatekeeper or a behavioral operating system?

## 5. States

- GREEN: technology increases agency, transparency and optionality.
- YELLOW: mixed technology with capture risks; requires governance and user discipline.
- RED: technology concentrates control over identity, money, information, attention or truth.

## 6. Interaction with investment research

This module can raise audit priority for sectors such as:

- AI platforms and model providers.
- Cybersecurity and identity.
- Payment rails and CBDC infrastructure.
- Social media and recommendation engines.
- Defense/surveillance vendors.
- Biotech/genomics.
- Space infrastructure.

It does not convert civilizational concerns into automatic investment conclusions. A risky technology can still produce investable companies, and an investable company can still create civilizational risk.

## 7. Conspiraciones Atlas protocol

When used inside Conspiraciones Atlas:

- Label facts as facts.
- Label hypotheses as hypotheses.
- Label interpretations as interpretations.
- Label speculation as speculation.
- Do not treat a narrative as true or false by default.
- Compare claims against primary evidence and demographic/historical data.

## 8. Decision safety

- Avoid techno-utopian certainty.
- Avoid conspiratorial certainty.
- Avoid assuming fictional parallels prove real-world intent.
- Use fiction as a map of incentives and failure modes, not as evidence by itself.
