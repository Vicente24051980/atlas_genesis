# BD-03 — Kāren-Pahlav → Kamsarakan — Bridge Destruction Test

**Status:** CLOSED-PARTIAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Frozen claim
`Kāren Pahlav → Kamsarakan` as a historical house-origin claim, with a stronger subclaim of recoverable genealogy through Pērōzmat.

This test deliberately separates:

- **BD-03A — house-origin claim:** Kamsarakan historically derives from / preserves an origin tradition in the Iranian Kāren Pahlav house.
- **BD-03B — pedigree claim:** a recoverable individual genealogy connects Kāren → Pērōzmat → Kamsarakan.

## Survival criterion
A house-origin claim may survive on convergent independent evidence of dynastic tradition, social location, nomenclature and external historical context. A pedigree claim requires actual genealogical bridging evidence and cannot be rescued by general cultural compatibility.

## Evidence audit
1. **Toumanoff / KAMSARAKAN (Encyclopaedia Iranica)** calls the Kamsarakan an offshoot of Kāren Pahlav and notes that the later surname Pahlavuni commemorated this claimed origin.
2. **Parvaneh Pourshariati / KĀRIN (Encyclopaedia Iranica)** independently treats the Kamsarakan as Armenian Kārins who traced their genealogy to the Iranian Kārin Pahlav family. This is stronger than mere repetition of Toumanoff because the article is centered on the Iranian Kārin house and tests Armenian tradition against Sasanian inscriptional evidence.
3. However, the specific individual **Pērōzmat** connecting the traditions is explicitly noted as **only attested by Movsēs Khorenacʿi**.
4. Contemporary Sasanian inscriptional evidence independently confirms the Kārin/Kāren as a real and important Iranian great house, but **contradicts part of Khorenatsi's narrative** that the Kāren were effectively destroyed by Ardašīr except for Pērōzmat. The Kāren appear among high nobility in early Sasanian inscriptions.
5. Garsoïan's broader work on Armeno-Iranian relations independently supports the historical importance of Iranian/Parthian great-house identities in Armenian aristocratic self-representation. This supports context and plausibility, not the specific pedigree.
6. The later use of `Pahlavuni` by Kamsarakan descendants is evidence of durable dynastic memory/identity claim. It is not by itself proof that every intermediate generation is recoverable or that the origin claim is literally genealogical in the strong sense.

## Source-risk analysis
Khorenatsi is indispensable for the named bridge through Pērōzmat, but:
- his dating is disputed;
- his aristocratic genealogies require patronage/bias controls;
- at least one major element of his Kāren narrative conflicts with contemporary Sasanian inscriptional evidence.

Therefore explicitness in Khorenatsi cannot upgrade BD-03B to a hard pedigree.

## Result matrix
- **Existence and status of Iranian Kāren house:** SURVIVES independently.
- **Kamsarakan tradition/identity of Kāren-Pahlav origin:** SURVIVES as a durable historical origin tradition; supported by scholarship independent of Toumanoff's Kamsarakan article.
- **Historical derivation of Kamsarakan from Kāren in a broad house-origin sense:** PARTIAL / plausible but not demonstrable as a continuous pedigree.
- **Pērōzmat as verified genealogical bridge:** DOCUMENTARY GAP; only Khorenatsi attests him in this role.
- **Continuous Kāren→Kamsarakan pedigree:** DOES NOT SURVIVE.
- **Khorenatsi massacre/single-survivor story:** materially challenged by contemporary Sasanian inscriptions showing the Kāren remained prominent.

## Canonical graph consequence
Allowed hard/soft representation:

- `Kamsarakan claims_descent_from Kāren-Pahlav` — YES, as a historical dynastic tradition.
- `Kamsarakan historiographically_derived_from Kāren-Pahlav` — YES only with `PARTIAL`/dispute metadata.
- `Kāren parent-line → Pērōzmat → Kamsarakan` — NO hard genealogy.

Do not use Pērōzmat as a bridge node without explicit source-risk metadata.

## Stop rule
BD-03 closes as `CLOSED-PARTIAL` under the present evidence. It may be reopened for new early Armenian, Middle Persian, epigraphic, sigillographic or other independent evidence that directly links a Kamsarakan ancestor to the Kāren house.

## Verdict
**The broad Kāren-Pahlav origin tradition survives; the continuous genealogy does not.**

This is the first Bridge Destruction test whose correct output is `PARTIAL`, demonstrating why origin tradition and pedigree must be different claim types.
