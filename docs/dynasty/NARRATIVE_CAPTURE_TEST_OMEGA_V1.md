# NARRATIVE CAPTURE TEST Ω v1

**Status:** EXECUTED — first comparative pass  
**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Frozen question
Does apparent superior Bagratuni antiquity/prestige persist after controlling for source author, patronage, date and documentary survival?

## Rival models
`H1 HISTORICAL NETWORK` vs `H0 GENEALOGICAL PROGRAM`.

## Controls
Author removal; source-date separation; patronage/source-interest flags; documentary-survival control; no raw-count scoring.

## Findings
- Xorenac'i is a positive control for narrative capture: modern criticism identifies strong pro-Bagratuni orientation and Mamikonean downgrading/substitution.
- Lazar is explicitly linked to the Mamikonean milieu and commissioned by Vahan; his family prominence is not neutral either.
- Epic Histories strongly foreground Mamikonean heroic structures.
- Ps.-Sebeos contains a laudatory Smbat Bagratuni block, but Smbat's importance also has independent documentary support.
- T'ovma is an explicit Artsruni dynastic-history source; remote pedigree density is programmatic unless externally controlled.

## Author-removal result
Removing Xorenac'i materially collapses Bagratuni **remote prestige/deep ancestry**, but not 6th–7th-century institutional prominence. Mamikonean prominence remains strong, though its principal narrative sources also have house-specific orientation.

## Decision
**NARRATIVE CAPTURE Ω v1 = PARTIAL POSITIVE.** Positive at author/program level; not established as a single corpus-wide Bagratuni capture.

## Hard consequence
No remote ancestry claim gains confidence by adjacency to an interested source. Cross-author persistence and external corroboration are required before prestige/depth enters the hard graph.
